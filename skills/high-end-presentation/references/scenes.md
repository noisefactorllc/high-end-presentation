# Scene archetypes

Use one archetype as the starting point for the brief's `scene` and `motion` fields.
Adapt the materials, light, and time of day to the piece's palette and themes.
The stage runner appends the fidelity clauses and camera clauses. Do not repeat them.

Rules for every scene text:

- Describe a photograph, not an illustration.
- Put the piece straight on, or at a gentle angle. A steep side view weakens the proportion check.
- Give the lens and distance. The lens sets the field of view; no later stage changes it.
- Make the piece fill 35 to 60 percent of the frame width. Smaller pieces fail the gate.
- Leave open floor in front of the piece, so people can pass it and stop to look.
- Say nothing about people in the `scene` text. The reference still must be empty.
- Do not ask for signs, boards, or anything with words. The runner forbids text in the still; image models add lettering whenever a scene invites it.

## gallery

Scene: "A photograph of a minimalist contemporary art gallery. The artwork hangs alone on a tall matte white wall as a large museum print in a thin black aluminum float frame, lit by one warm-neutral track spotlight from above. Polished pale concrete floor. Cool daylight from a tall window out of frame. Shot on a 50mm lens from about six meters, at eye level, straight on; the framed artwork fills about half of the frame width."

Motion: "Visitors wander through the gallery at an unhurried pace. Two of them stop beside the artwork and look at it for a long moment. Daylight shifts slowly across the floor."

Light choices: warm spot on cool daylight for dark or metallic pieces; soft north light for pale or pastel pieces.

## home

Scene: "A photograph of a calm, lived-in modern living room. The artwork hangs above a low walnut sideboard with a ceramic vase and a stack of books, in a slim oak frame with a white mat. Late afternoon sun through sheer curtains throws soft window light across the wall. Shot on a 35mm lens at standing eye level, straight on; the framed artwork fills about forty percent of the frame width."

Motion: "People of the household move through the room: someone crosses with a cup of coffee, someone pauses at the sideboard and looks up at the artwork. Curtain shadows sway gently on the wall."

Light choices: warm afternoon for warm palettes; overcast daylight for cool palettes.

## storefront

Scene: "A photograph taken at night from a city sidewalk, looking straight at the tall plate-glass window of a dark, minimal gallery. Behind the glass, the artwork hangs alone on a charcoal wall in a slim black frame, lit so it glows. The glass carries faint reflections of warm streetlights. The wet sidewalk shines in the foreground. Shot on a 35mm lens at eye level from across the sidewalk; the artwork fills about forty percent of the frame width."

Motion: "An evening crowd streams along the sidewalk in both directions between the camera and the window. Headlights from the street behind the camera sweep reflections across the glass and the wet pavement. A couple stops at the window to look at the artwork."

Light choices: night for trails (lively, bustling); blue hour for calmer energy. Headlights, bicycle lamps, and lit phones make the best light trails.

## modern-frame

Scene: "A photograph of the artwork in a deep, gallery-grade shadow-box frame of matte black steel, hung on a smooth plaster wall of a warm grey tone in a quiet architect's studio. A single raking light from the upper left grazes the wall and frame. Shot on an 85mm lens from about four meters, straight on; the framed artwork fills about sixty percent of the frame width."

Motion: "Now and then someone walks through the studio behind the camera and their soft shadow passes across the wall. One person stops beside the frame to study the artwork."

Light choices: raking light suits textured or high-contrast pieces; flat, even light suits graphic pieces.

## glass-display

For a moving-image piece, or any piece presented as a digital object.

Scene: "A photograph of a self-contained display: the artwork glows inside a thick, frameless block of optical glass standing upright on a pale stone plinth in a dim, quiet exhibition room. The glass edges catch thin lines of light. The glow of the artwork spills softly onto the plinth. Shot on a 50mm lens at the height of the display, straight on; the glass block fills about half of the frame width."

Motion: "Visitors drift through the dim room. Their silhouettes pass behind and beside the plinth; one leans in to look closely at the display, lit by its glow, then steps back."

Light choices: keep the room darker than the piece so the piece reads as the light source.

## screens

For glowing, saturated, or moving pieces shown as displays in a busy public space. Several pieces work well here as a row or a wall of screens.

Scene: "A photograph of a busy international airport concourse at night. The artworks play on large, bright, frameless LCD screens mounted side by side on a dark wall above the concourse, glowing intensely. Their colored light spills across the polished terrazzo floor, the glass balustrade, and the brushed-steel columns. Distant blue ceiling lights glow down the concourse. Shot on a 35mm lens at eye level from across the concourse, straight on; the row of screens fills about seventy percent of the frame width."

Motion: "Travelers stream through the concourse in both directions, pulling rolling suitcases, some hurrying, some stopping to look up at the screens. An electric cart glides past with its lights on. The screens' colored light washes over the people as they pass."

Light choices: keep the venue darker than the screens so the screens are the main light sources; polished floors and glass carry their colors through the scene. A mall atrium or a transit hall works the same way.
